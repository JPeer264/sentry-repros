`guides/react/features/error-boundary.mdx:21-53` shows a React 19 root with `onCaughtError: Sentry.reactErrorHandler()` next to `Sentry.ErrorBoundary`, then documents `beforeCapture` tags (`:151`) and `captureReactException` with the linked component stack (`:173`).

Observed with the page's own `main.jsx`: every boundary error is captured first by the root hook (mechanism `auto.function.react.error_handler`, `handled: false`), and the boundary's own capture, the `beforeCapture` tags and `captureReactException` are dropped by the dedupe integration, five times `Not capturing exception because it's already been captured`. On a plain `createRoot` without the hooks the boundary path works as described: `auto.function.react.error_boundary`, `handled: true`, `tags.section` set, component stack linked.

Related, on the guide itself: `guides/react/index.mdx:163-167` passes a callback to `onUncaughtError: Sentry.reactErrorHandler((error, errorInfo) => {...})`; the render error that unmounted the whole root then arrives with `handled: true`, because passing a callback marks the error as handled, while the callback-less `onCaughtError` hook reports boundary-caught errors as `handled: false`.

- State that with `onCaughtError` set, the ErrorBoundary's `beforeCapture` and `captureReactException` never run, and tell readers who rely on them to leave `onCaughtError` out or to tag inside its callback.
- Say what the `handled` flag means for each hook, and drop the `console.warn` callback from the `onUncaughtError` snippet or explain its effect.
